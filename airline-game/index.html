✈️ Airline Pricing Game

You manage ticket prices for one airline on a route with a single competitor. Your goal is to maximize profit.

The setup

1,200 seatsyou, and 1,200 for your rival
3 booking phases3 months, 1 month, 5 days before departure
€4variable cost per passenger (airport tax)
€48,380fixed cost of flying the plane

If both airlines charge €50 in every phase, each sells about 1,100 seats.
Leisure travelers book early and are very price-sensitive. Business travelers book late and care much less about price. You set one price per phase. The rival sets its own price at the same time, and you see it only after the phase ends. Seats you sell early are no longer available later.

What's going on?
  • Price discrimination over time: early buyers are mostly leisure travelers, whose demand is elastic, so the price should be low. Late buyers are mostly business travelers, whose demand is inelastic, so the price should be high.
  • Capacity is the twist: each seat sold cheaply to a leisure traveler is one seat you can't sell later to a business traveler.
  • Becoming the monopolist: if you let your rival sell out its seats in the first two phases, it has no seats left in the last phase. You are then the only airline selling to business travelers, who are willing to pay a lot.
